Our Story

About the University

For over 177 years, Stonebridge University has stood as one of England's finest institutions for the study of business, commerce, and economics.

Our Heritage

A History of Excellence

Stonebridge University was founded in 1847 by a consortium of Oxford merchants and industrialists who recognised the need for a dedicated institution to educate the commercial and financial leaders of the Victorian era.

From its original home in Wellington Hall — a Grade I listed building at the heart of Oxford — the School has grown from a modest college of commerce into one of Europe's leading research-intensive business schools, holding triple accreditation from AACSB, AMBA, and EQUIS.

Our graduates have founded banks, led central banking institutions, shaped economic policy, and built some of the world's most recognisable commercial enterprises. That tradition continues today through world-class teaching, cutting-edge research, and an uncompromising commitment to academic rigour.

1847
School of Commerce founded in Wellington Hall, Oxford, by the Stonebridge Merchant Society.
1902
Department of Finance & Banking established; first endowed Chair of Political Economy awarded.
1948
Granted full University status. Launch of the first MBA programme in the United Kingdom.
1972
School renamed Stonebridge University School of Business & Economics; first female Dean appointed.
1998
AACSB accreditation achieved. Stonebridge becomes one of only a handful of UK schools to hold the designation.
2009
Triple accreditation (AACSB, AMBA, EQUIS) secured — joining the top 1% of business schools globally.
2025
Rebranded as Stonebridge University School of Business & Finance, reflecting our deepened focus on financial scholarship.
